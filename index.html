⊙ Step 1 · Water Source & Site
The water source defines static head, sustainable yield, suction limits, and source storage — the inputs that dominate pump TDH and flow feasibility.
Start by telling the tool where the water comes from. This decides whether the pump can lift enough water and whether the source can refill fast enough.
Plain language: static water level means how far down the water is before pumping starts. Pumping water level means how far down it falls while the pump is running. The pump must be strong enough for the lower level, not only the resting level.
Technical: use PWL/dynamic level for design head. SWL is shown for interpretation, but TDH should include drawdown, delivery head, pipe friction, and operating pressure.

⌁ Field Test 1 — String & Weight (measure SWL)
▶
Tie a weight to the end of a measuring tape or marked rope, lower it down the borehole until you hear a splash or feel slack, then mark the line at the wellhead. The depth from wellhead to the wet line is your static water level.

⏱ Field Test 2 — Bucket & Stopwatch (measure yield)
▶
Run the existing pump (or any pump) into a bucket of known volume. Time how long the bucket takes to fill. Repeat 3 times and use the average. This gives the sustainable yield at that operating point.

▼ Field Test 3 — Drawdown Timing (estimate PWL)
▶
Start the pump and measure the water level (using the string-and-weight method) at intervals. When the level stops dropping (usually 30–60 min), that's the pumping water level for that pumping rate. Enter the stable level and the rate.

Yield warning. For shallow wells, the well yield (not the head) is usually the limit. A pump rated faster than the well refills will run dry. Standing volume in well ≈ — L; that drains in — at the chosen pumping rate. Surface or low-lift submersible pumps suit shallow wells best.
The same field-test helpers above (string & weight, bucket & stopwatch) work for shallow wells. Switch to Borehole temporarily to use them, then come back.
Suction lift cap. Surface pumps cannot lift water more than ~7 m by suction (atmospheric pressure limit ~10 m, less friction and cavitation margin). If suction lift exceeds 7 m, use a submersible or floating pump instead.
Gravity head only. 1 m of tank height ≈ 0.1 bar of pressure at the field. Drip emitters need ~1 bar, so tank-fed systems usually work for hose/basin/furrow but may not give enough pressure for standard drip — consider low-pressure drip tape (rated 0.3–0.5 bar).
With a raised tank as the source, the irrigation pump is small or unnecessary. The system you size will be the refill pump from the source above to the tank.
Annual harvestable yield ≈ — L/yr (= area × rainfall × runoff coefficient). Daily wet-season average ≈ — L/day. Dry-season storage requirement is the binding constraint — see Step 4 for tank sizing.
Seasonality. Roof catchment alone rarely meets full irrigation demand. It is best as a supplementary source paired with a borehole or shallow well.
▦ Step 2 · Bed Register
Register each hydraulic management unit. Each bed/cluster carries its own crop coefficient, root depth, application efficiency, soil intake class, and event runtime.
Enter each bed or bag cluster separately. The output tells the operator how many litres to apply, how often, and for how long to run the hose or pipe.
What is a bund? A bund is the small raised edge around a bed that holds water in place when the bed is flooded. If the required water depth is higher than the bund, water spills out and the bed should be split or watered in two rounds.
Bund height: used as the maximum ponding depth for basin and hose-to-basin events. The tool flags overtopping when gross event depth approaches or exceeds the bund.

⊞ Step 3 · Pump Selection
Translates the bed schedule into required flow and head, then ranks SunCulture pump options against the operating point.
Field flow calibration · bucket test
Use this when catalogue flow is uncertain. Fill a known bucket from the hose/outlet and let the tool calculate the real stream size.
Measured outlet flow overrides assumed stream size for bed runtimes and required pump flow. Formula: Q(L/s)=bucket volume ÷ fill time.
—
Plain language: this tells us how fast water actually comes out of the hose. It makes the bed runtimes more believable than using only catalogue values.
Required flow: how fast the pump must deliver water so the farmer can finish all beds within the available irrigation hours.
Total head: the uphill lift plus pipe losses. Higher head means the same pump gives less water.
Catalogue fit: a pump fits only if it can meet both the flow and the head.
Required Q: peak daily farm demand divided by operating hours; used with TDH to filter pump envelopes.
TDH: static/dynamic source lift + slope-derived field elevation head + friction head + operating pressure/delivery head.
Oversizing penalty: candidates are ranked by fit envelope and distance from required operating point.

Checks whether the water source can keep up with the irrigation plan during the dry season.

Plain language: a pump can be strong enough and still be dangerous if the well cannot refill. Watch recovery time, falling water levels, and dry spells.
Technical: groundwater reliability combines safe yield/demand ratio, drawdown, recovery, rainfall anomaly/dry days, dry-season length and water-level trend.
